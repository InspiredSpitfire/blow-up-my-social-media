# Build backward from retention

Working model [I]: on the video-led platforms (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook Reels) and on LinkedIn, distribution follows whether people stayed and then did something. So the piece is designed from the end to the start, and the hook is the last thing written.

**X is the carve-out.** Its published weights put dwell near the bottom (0.05) and replies, quotes, and sends at the top. On X the spine is the reply or the send: write the ending as the line someone has to answer or forward, and build backward from that.

## The order

1. **Outcome.** Pick one: follow, comment, share, save, or sale.
2. **The ending.** Write the last line or last frame. What must the viewer know or feel in that moment for the outcome to be the obvious next move? (Table below.)
3. **The payoff.** The thing the piece delivers. It must be real, specific, and complete without clicking anything.
4. **The middle.** List only the beats required to earn the payoff. Each beat opens with its own mini-promise (a re-hook) and closes its loop before the next one opens. Anything that doesn't move toward the payoff is cut: "so basically", recaps, throat-clearing, the logo.
5. **The hook.** Now write it, as an honest one-line promise of the payoff for a named person. Write 5 to 10, keep one, keep the runner-up for the other platform or the repost.
6. **Packaging.** Cover, title, thumbnail, first line. It raises the question the hook starts answering.
7. **The conversation plan.** Who replies to which early comments, and the question that keeps threads alive. This serves the conversation; no platform here has confirmed a first-hour ranking window.
8. **The measurement.** Retention first (3-second hold, average view duration, read-through, swipe-through), then the outcome metric. If retention is broken, the outcome metric is not diagnosable yet.

## What earns each outcome

| Outcome | What the ending has to do | What the body has to contain | Weak version to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follow** | Name the recurring promise: there is more of exactly this, and when | Proof this is a series or a point of view, not a one-off | "Follow for more" with no "more" defined |
| **Comment** | Ask for something only the viewer has: their number, their version, their worst example | A take with a reason, so there is something to answer | "Thoughts?" "Agree?" and keyword bait where the platform demotes it |
| **Share / send** | Land a line that is "so them" it has to be sent to one specific person | A specific person's specific situation, named | Broad relatability that is about nobody |
| **Save** | Tell them when they will need this again | Reference value: a framework, checklist, script, or steps | A list of things they already know |
| **Sale** | One action, stated once, on a route that works on this platform | Proof first, then the offer, then who it is for and who it isn't | An ad with no value in it, or four asks at once |

## Hook tests

- Would the named person stop on this line?
- Does the piece deliver exactly this promise?
- Could a competitor post it unchanged? If yes, sharpen it with the detail only this brand has.

Hook shapes that hold up (the shape, not a script): result with an unexpected method; contrarian claim with a reason; a specific real number; a mistake confessed; a callout that names the person; the outcome shown first; real stakes; opening mid-story; a list where the last item is the reason to stay; the wrong way shown for one second.

## Retention tools

- Promise early, pay off late: state the payoff in the first tenth, deliver it in the last fifth.
- Change something every 5 to 8 seconds in short video, every 30 to 60 seconds in long video, every 3 to 4 lines in text. [I]
- The button: end on a line that lands (callback, reversal, punchline), then the CTA. In looping video formats, a last line that flows into the first earns replays.
- Show the mistake. A build with the error left in holds attention because the viewer wants to see the fix.

## Testing discipline

One variable at a time: hook, cover, or CTA. Keep a per-brand list of hooks that held attention and reuse the pattern, never the words. When results come back, read retention before anything else.
