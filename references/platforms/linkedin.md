## How distribution works now

- Production ranking is LLM-based retrieval plus a generative recommender that predicts click, skip, long dwell, like, comment, and share from a member's long interaction history. [P1] "360Brew" is not the production ranker; LinkedIn says it was tested and shut down. [P2]
- Out-of-network reach is matched by **topic**. The profile headline and About section feed that matching, so a profile and a posting history that stay on a few topics help. [P1]
- Dwell is a confirmed signal. A "first 60 to 90 minutes" window is folklore; LinkedIn has never published one. [P1] Replying to early comments is still good manners and good conversation. Don't promise it moves ranking.
- LinkedIn named "Comment 'Yes' if you agree" style posts for reduced reach and says it is curbing comment automation through third-party tools and extensions. [P3] Bots and extensions that automate activity are banned. [P4]
- Saves and sends are reported in analytics (since 2025-09). They are not confirmed ranking objectives. [D5] Comment impressions are also reported. [D6]

## Formats

- **Text posts.** Best engagement at 1,301 to 2,500 characters; under 400 does worst (372k personal-profile posts, Sep 2025 to Feb 2026). [D7] The ~140 mobile / ~210 desktop cut before "see more" is convention, unpublished by LinkedIn. [I] Line one is the hook, line two earns the click.
- **Documents (PDF carousels).** Top format by engagement rate, 7.00% across 1.3M page posts. [D8] Nobody has measured dwell by format; don't claim it. Cover slide is a hook, one idea per slide, CTA and recap on the last.
- **Video.** Lost its edge: views down 36% year over year across page sizes [D8], the Video tab was tested for removal [D9], and video paired with unrelated text gets no bonus [P3]. Use video when the footage is the proof. Native upload, captions, hook in 2 seconds.
- **Hashtags.** Hashtag following is gone; staff call them "nice to have". Use 0 to 3. Eight to fifteen reads as spam. [D10]

## Links

LinkedIn staff say there is no intentional link penalty if the post stands on its own. [D10] Third-party data still shows a 15 to 36% gap, mostly on company pages, and finds first-comment links get far fewer clicks. [D11] Default: if the click is the goal, put the link in the post and make the post worth reading without it. "Link in first comment" is no longer the safe default. The "80% penalty for links in comments" claim has no source.

## Cadence and timing

3 to 8pm weekdays is now the best window, Wednesday 4pm on top; mornings are below average (4.8M posts). [D12] Six to ten posts a week is associated with higher impressions per post, and consistency matters more than volume. [D12] Use the account's own analytics over any benchmark when they exist.

## Comment-gates and DMs

**Do not use "comment WORD and I'll send it" here.** It is the named demotion pattern [P3] and there is no automation-safe way to fulfil it [P4]. Use instead: a real question, "DM me WORD", a poll, or the link in the post. All fulfilment is manual.

## Converting

LinkedIn converts to conversations and calls. Post → profile → DM or Featured link. Headline states who is helped and the result. Featured carries the one link. About opens on the reader's problem. Never pitch in the first DM: reply to what they said, ask one question, then offer. Newsletter claims: unverified, test before relying. [I]
