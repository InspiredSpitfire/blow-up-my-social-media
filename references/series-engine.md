# Series engine: audience, angles, series, calendar

An account people recognize is a small number of repeatable series, each with a promise. One-off posts earn views. Series earn follows, because the follow is a subscription to the next episode.

## The research pass (run it for a brand once, refresh quarterly)

Deliver all four parts. Label every part FACT (from the user's comments, DMs, surveys, sales calls, analytics), INFERENCE (from the brand kit and the niche), or UNAVAILABLE. Ask for real audience language first; if there is none, say so and proceed on inference.

1. **The audience's 10 most urgent questions, frustrations, and desired outcomes.** In their words, not category labels. Balance the three; desired outcomes drive the offers.
2. **The overused advice in the niche.** The lines every competitor posts. This is the list of things the brand never says straight.
3. **10 defensible contrarian angles.** Each is an opinion or a lived-experience claim with a receipt the brand actually has. None states or implies a statistic, a guaranteed result, a medical, legal, or financial claim, or anything about a named competitor. Test each: could a hostile commenter fairly call this exaggerated? If yes, narrow it until they can't.
4. **5 repeatable series.** For each:
   - Series name (in the brand's voice, from the kit)
   - Audience promise (one sentence; what they get every episode)
   - Ideal format and platform (from the platform files, not habit)
   - 10 episode ideas, each specific enough that no generic account could post it unchanged
   - Why someone would follow after one post
   - What proof or personal experience to show
   - The offer it points to, or "none"

Banned as series or episodes unless made unusually specific to this audience: "tips", "motivation", "behind the scenes", "day in the life", "my journey".

## Honesty rules

- Anything stated about the person's real life or work is marked **(confirm)** until they confirm it. First-person episode titles are drafts.
- Proof means something that can be shown on screen: the file, the screen, the receipt, the error.
- Roast systems and advice, never the reader and never a named person.
- Placements on a platform with no audience data are labeled as tests.

## Give each series one job

| Job | Series shape that does it |
|---|---|
| Follows and reach | A system with parts (each episode reveals one), or a numbered point-of-view series |
| Shares and sends | "This is so you" scenarios, roasts of a shared enemy with a fix |
| Saves | Reference carousels and documents they will need again |
| Trust | Real builds with the mistakes left in |
| Sales | Whichever series carries proof of the thing being sold; at least one series never sells |

Use Instagram's linked Reels series and YouTube playlists so the platform itself connects episodes.

## Turning series into a calendar

- Pick the primary platform from where the audience already is (the account's own numbers), not from where the format is trendy.
- Start from a cadence the person can sustain on a bad week. Completion beats volume. Batch on high-energy days and schedule the rest.
- A week mixes jobs: mostly reach and trust, some saves, and roughly one ask for every three to five value pieces. [I] During a launch, go closer to one to one and say it is launch week.
- Each calendar row: date, platform, series and episode, job, hook, format, CTA, asset needed, status.
- A week built around **one offer** with hand-raiser posts in it belongs to the `hand-raiser` skill.

## After it runs

Ask for the numbers per series, not per post: retention, the job metric (follows, sends, saves, comments, clicks), and sales. Kill or rework the series that fails its job over 6 to 8 episodes. Keep the ones that do their job even when they are not the view leaders.
