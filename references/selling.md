# Selling merch and digital assets from social

Engagement with no path to action is expensive entertainment. Design the path before the post. Never promise sales, invent scarcity, fake a deadline, or make up a testimonial.

## Offer clarity first (one line each)

Who it is for. What they get (the deliverable). The honest result and by when. Price and friction. The real reason now, or none (then use a standing CTA).

## The path

```
Reach content → Trust content (proof) → Ask → Route that works on this platform → Page that repeats the promise word for word → Action
```

Find the first broken arrow and fix only that. 20,000 views and 4 clicks is a CTA or profile problem. 400 visits and 6 opt-ins is a page problem.

## Where a click can actually happen (Verified 2026-09-20; details in each platform file)

| Platform | Routes that work | Routes that don't |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Link in the post (post must stand alone), Featured section, manual DM | "Comment WORD" gates, any automation |
| X | Link in post (test against a no-link version), profile link | Automated keyword DMs |
| Instagram | Bio link (current offer on top), Story link sticker, comment-to-DM within Meta's rules, broadcast channel (eligibility unverified; check the account) | In-app checkout (gone), caption links (Meta Verified test only) |

| YouTube Shorts | Related-video link, channel profile links | Links in Shorts descriptions and comments (not clickable) |

## Merch

- Merch sells on identity and inside jokes, so it sells from series people already follow. Show it worn or used, in the content, before it is ever pitched.
- YouTube Shopping needs the Partner Program; Fourthwall is reported as a supported platform [D]. The subscriber threshold is unverified, so check the channel's Studio. Outside the program, merch routes are the bio or profile link and Stories.
- Drops with a real window beat an always-open store for attention. The window must be real.
- Show the design process and the rejected versions. That is trust content that happens to feature product.

## Digital assets (downloads, prompt packs, templates, toolkits, plugins, workshops)

- The best lead magnet or low-ticket product **extends the piece they just watched**: the checklist from this video, the prompt behind this reel, the template from this carousel.
- Lead a gate with the asset, not the discount. A discount can ride second.
- Show the asset working on screen. A working demo is the proof.
- Price decides the format. Free and under about $50: short-form, Stories, comment-to-DM where allowed. $50 to $500: carousel or long-form proof plus a direct ask. Above that: long-form or reasoning posts → conversation → call. [I]
- No platform here has a good native checkout for digital goods for small accounts. Own the page.

## Mechanics worth knowing [I] (not ranked; nobody has published comparative data; feature availability is not source-checked, so confirm on the account)

- Comment-to-DM where the platform allows it, with fulfilment ready before the post goes live.
- Story sequence with a link sticker for time-boxed offers: problem → proof → offer → link, 4 to 6 frames, a poll early.
- Direct-response post: hook that names the pain, proof, the offer, who it is for, one action.
- Long-form "how it works" video with the link pinned, for anything that needs trust.
- Live with a pinned offer, for launches; the replay becomes content.
- Challenge or event (LinkedIn Events, Facebook Events) for a warm list with a real reason now.
- DM conversation after engagement: reply to what they said, ask one question, then offer. Never pitch first.
- Collab posts and pinned posts on Instagram; a profile that states the result and carries one current link everywhere.

## Asks

One CTA per piece. Say the action, not the feature. Place it right after the payoff, spoken and on screen. Tell them exactly what happens next. Roughly one ask for every three to five value pieces, with Stories, DMs, and pinned comments carrying most of the selling. [I]

## Measure

Reach → profile visits → link clicks or DM starts → page views → opt-ins or purchases. For gates: comments received vs fulfilled vs clicked. Label anything you were not given as unavailable. Views, profile visits, and follower counts are never reported as a conversion rate.
